AoA triangulation

One receiver site — like your 4-antenna interferometer — gives a direction, not a position: the emitter is somewhere along that bearing line. Two sites give two lines, and lines cross at one place. Add more sites and the lines rarely agree perfectly, so the answer becomes a best fit rather than a single crossing.

Play with the geometry: with the emitter far away, even ±2° wedges overlap in a huge diamond — angle error grows into position error with distance. Two sites whose bearings cross at a shallow angle give a badly stretched fix; crossing near 90° is ideal. Adding a third, fourth, or fifth site shrinks the overlap region fast, which is why real networks favour many cheap sites over one perfect one.
