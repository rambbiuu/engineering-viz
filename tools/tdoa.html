TDoA geolocation

The other big way to find an emitter — no antenna arrays, no bearings. Three stations timestamp the same signal; it arrives earliest at the nearest one. Each pair's arrival-time difference draws a curve of possible positions (a hyperbola), and the curves cross at one point: the emitter. Map is 10 × 6 km.

Compare with AoA triangulation: TDoA needs no knowledge of the signal's frequency and no antenna spacing tricks — but it demands the three stations share clocks synchronized to nanoseconds (1 µs of clock error = 300 m of position error), and a signal with sharp timing structure to timestamp. AoA needs neither, but needs the phase-coherent array you have been studying. Real systems often run both and fuse the answers.
