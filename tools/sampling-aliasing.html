Sampling and aliasing

A digital receiver never sees the whole wave — only snapshots taken fₛ times per second. Take them fast enough (more than twice the signal frequency, the Nyquist rule) and the snapshots pin down the true wave. Too slow, and a fake low-frequency ghost fits the same dots.

Try it: leave the signal at 3 Hz and drag the sample rate down below 6 Sa/s — watch the dashed ghost appear and the spike jump inside the shaded zone. This is why your 2–18 GHz receiver either needs a blisteringly fast ADC or must mix the signal down to a lower frequency first: an emitter above the Nyquist limit does not disappear, it shows up disguised at the wrong frequency.
