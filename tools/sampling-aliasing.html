        The arithmetic, with the current numbers

          The only cure is a filter before the converter: once folded, the impostor and the original are byte-for-byte identical.

          Glossary
          Sample
          One measurement of the wave's height. Between samples the converter knows nothing.
          Sample rate, fs
          Samples taken each second, in samples per second (Sa/s).
          Sampling period, Ts
          Time between two samples, 1 ÷ fs, in seconds.
          Nyquist rate
          Twice the highest frequency present, 2f. The sample rate must be strictly greater.
          Nyquist limit
          Half the sample rate, fs / 2. The highest frequency a rate can carry honestly.
          Aliasing
          A frequency above the limit appearing as a different, lower one. The samples of the two are identical.
          Apparent frequency
          Distance from f to the nearest whole multiple of fs.
          Anti-alias filter
          A low-pass filter before the converter, so nothing above the limit can fold.
          Reconstruction filter
          A low-pass filter after the converter, cutting at the limit to remove the images.
          Zero-order hold
          Holding each sample steady until the next arrives, producing a staircase.
          Sinc interpolation
          The ideal join: each sample becomes sinc(u) = sin(πu) ÷ (πu), and the curves add.
          Image
          A copy of the spectrum that sampling creates at every whole multiple of fs.
          Folding
          The frequency axis creased at every multiple of fs / 2 and folded back like an accordion.
          Wagon-wheel effect
          Wheels running backwards on film. The camera is a strobe at 24 flashes a second.
          Hold droop
          Amplitude loss from a zero-order hold, a factor sinc(fa / fs), plus half a sample of delay.
          Oversampling ratio
          fs divided by 2f. Above 1.00 works; 1.00 exactly does not.
