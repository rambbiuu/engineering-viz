Phase interferometry

One equation runs everything: Δφ = 360° · d · sin θ / λ, wrapped to ±180°. Short baselines are coarse but honest; long ones are precise but hide whole cycles.

Recovered angle of arrival — —
Worked example — 2 GHz, 30°, d = 2.4 cm

λ = 29.98 / 2 = 14.99 cm. Δφ = 360° · 2.4 · sin 30° / 14.99 = 28.8°. Inverting: sin θ = 28.8 · 14.99 / (360 · 2.4) = 0.500, so θ = 30.0°. Because 2.4 cm ≤ λ/2 = 7.5 cm, no whole cycle can hide, and the wrapped reading is the whole story. Above 6.25 GHz that stops being true even for 2.4 cm.

Glossary
Angle of arrival (θ)
The direction the signal comes from, measured from straight ahead.
Baseline (d)
The distance from the reference antenna A1 to another antenna.
Wavelength (λ)
The distance between two wave peaks; λ = c / f.
Phase difference (Δφ)
How much later the wave arrives at one antenna than at A1, in degrees.
Wrapping
A receiver reads phase only modulo 360°; whole cycles vanish from the reading.
Ambiguity
When d > λ/2, several different angles produce the same wrapped phase.
Geolocation
Working backwards from the measured phases to the emitter's direction.
