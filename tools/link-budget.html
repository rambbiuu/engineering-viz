Worked example, with the current numbers
    What the sliders are teaching

    Doubling the distance costs 6 dB and doubling the frequency costs another 6 — both are the 20·log terms, and neither can be argued with. Antenna gain is free power at both ends, which is why a dish at each end beats a bigger amplifier. The noise floor sets the finish line: widen the receiver bandwidth and it rises 3 dB per doubling, so a link that fails on a wideband waveform may pass on a narrow one at the same power.

    Glossary
    dB
    A ratio on a log scale; +3 dB doubles the power, +10 dB is ten times.
    dBm
    Power compared with 1 milliwatt, in dB. 0 dBm = 1 mW; 20 dBm = 100 mW.
    dBi
    Antenna gain compared with an isotropic antenna that radiates equally everywhere.
    EIRP
    Effective isotropic radiated power: transmit power minus feeder loss plus antenna gain.
    Free-space loss
    Spreading loss with nothing in the way: 92.4 + 20·log fGHz + 20·log dkm dB.
    Feeder loss
    Power lost in the cable between radio and antenna.
    Noise figure
    How much noise the receiver adds on top of the unavoidable thermal noise, in dB.
    Noise floor
    N0 + 10·log B + NF. N0 is −174 dBm/Hz at 290 K.
    C/N
    Carrier-to-noise ratio: received power minus the noise floor, in dB.
    Fade margin
    How far the achieved C/N sits above the required C/N; headroom for a bad day.
