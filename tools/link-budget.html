    What the sliders are teaching

    Three lessons live in these sliders. Doubling the distance costs 6 dB and doubling the frequency costs another 6 — both are the 20log terms, and neither can be argued with. Antenna gain is free power in both directions, which is why a dish at each end beats a bigger amplifier. And the noise floor, kTB + noise figure, sets the finish line: widen the receiver bandwidth and the floor rises 3 dB per doubling, so a link that fails on a wideband waveform may pass on a narrow one at the same power.

    Glossary
    dB
    A ratio on a log scale; +3 dB doubles the power, +10 dB is ten times.
    dBm
    Power compared with 1 milliwatt, in dB. 0 dBm = 1 mW; 20 dBm = 100 mW.
    dBi
    Antenna gain compared with an isotropic antenna that radiates equally everywhere.
    EIRP
    Effective isotropic radiated power: transmit power minus feeder loss plus antenna gain.
    Free-space loss
    Spreading loss over distance with nothing in the way: 92.4 + 20log fGHz + 20log dkm dB.
    Feeder loss
    Power lost in the cable between radio and antenna.
    Noise figure
    How much noise the receiver adds on top of the unavoidable thermal noise, in dB.
    Noise floor
    kTB plus the noise figure: −174 dBm/Hz plus 10log of the bandwidth plus NF.
    C/N
    Carrier-to-noise ratio: received power minus the noise floor, in dB.
    Fade margin
    How far the achieved C/N sits above the required C/N; headroom for a bad day.
