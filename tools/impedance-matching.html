The arithmetic, with this antenna's numbers

    VSWR 2 is 9.54 dB of return loss but only 0.51 dB of mismatch loss — the two are not the same figure. The antenna is modelled as one resistance plus one reactive part, and mismatch loss ignores what the returned power does at the amplifier.

    Glossary
    Impedance Z, in Ω
    Voltage demanded per unit current, written R + jX. R absorbs power; X only stores and returns it.
    Reactance X, in Ω
    The storing part. Positive behaves like an inductor, negative like a capacitor.
    Characteristic impedance Z0
    What the cable wants to see at its far end. 50 Ω for radio, 75 Ω for video.
    Normalised impedance z
    Z / Z0, so z = 1 is a perfect match. The chart is drawn in these units.
    Reflection coefficient Γ
    Γ = (ZL − Z0) / (ZL + Z0), an amplitude fraction with a phase.
    VSWR
    Voltage standing wave ratio, Vmax / Vmin on the cable. 1 is perfect and it never goes below 1.
    Return loss, in dB
    How far below the outgoing wave the reflection sits, quoted positive. 20 dB is good, 6 dB is poor.
    Mismatch loss, in dB
    Transmitter power that never entered the antenna because it was reflected.
    Admittance Y, susceptance B
    Y = 1 / Z, in siemens. Shunt parts add susceptance, which is why they are easier to think about in Y.
    Series and shunt
    A series part sits in line and adds reactance. A shunt part sits across the line to ground.
    Electrical length, in λ
    Cable length measured in wavelengths. Half a wavelength takes the impedance once round the chart.
    L-network
    One shunt part and one series part. Its Q, and so its bandwidth, is fixed by the resistance ratio it bridges.
    Q
    How sharply tuned. Q = √(Rhigh/Rlow − 1), and the match holds over roughly f0/Q.
