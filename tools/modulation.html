Modulation playground

A carrier wave carries no information until you change something about it. There are only three things you can change — height (amplitude), spacing (frequency), or timing (phase) — and every modulation scheme, analog or digital, is one of those three moves.

The analog schemes (AM, FM) copy a smooth message onto the carrier; the digital ones (OOK, FSK, BPSK) snap between discrete states per bit. Notice the family resemblance: OOK is just extreme AM, FSK is FM with only two frequencies, and BPSK — flipping phase 180° per bit — is the move your interferometer measures in reverse: it reads phase, BPSK writes it.
