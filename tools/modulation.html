The arithmetic, with the current numbers
    The family resemblance, and what is simplified

    The analogue schemes (AM, FM, PM) copy a smooth message onto the carrier; the digital ones snap between discrete states once per bit or symbol. OOK is extreme AM, FSK is FM with two message levels, and BPSK — flipping phase 180° per bit — is the move an interferometer measures in reverse: it reads phase, BPSK writes it.

    Simplified here: no pulse shaping, so the digital spectra show the full sin x ÷ x skirts of rectangular keying; bandwidth is the Carson or null-to-null estimate, not a measured −3 dB width; the spectrum is a 512-point transform of one 1 s window with a Hann taper.

    Glossary
    Carrier
    The high-frequency wave that transports the message.
    Amplitude
    The height of the wave.
    Frequency
    How many wave cycles fit in one second.
    Phase
    Where the wave is in its cycle at a given instant.
    Envelope
    The outline traced by the wave's peaks.
    Keying
    Digital modulation: snapping between discrete states, one per bit or symbol.
    Deviation Δf
    How far FM or FSK pushes the instantaneous frequency away from the carrier.
    Carson's rule
    Bandwidth estimate for FM: B = 2 · (Δf + fm).
    Occupied bandwidth
    The span of frequency the transmission fills, in hertz.
    Spectral efficiency
    Bits per second carried per hertz of bandwidth.
