The arithmetic, on the numbers now on screen
    Three numbers worth remembering
    • 45° between two linear antennas costs 3.01 dB; 85° costs 21.19 dB.
    • Circular into linear loses 3.01 dB at every angle. That is the trade.
    • A real antenna's null stops at its XPD, 25 to 35 dB, never at infinity.
    • A letterbox slot only takes a letter held the right way round.
    Glossary
    Polarisation
    The direction the electric field points, seen across the beam. Set by the antenna that launched the wave.
    Linear, circular, elliptical
    The three shapes the field tip can trace: a straight line, a circle, or an ellipse between them.
    Ex and Ey
    The horizontal and vertical halves of the field. Any polarisation is these two added with some phase difference.
    Phase difference δ
    How far the vertical half lags or leads the horizontal half, as an angle. One whole cycle is 360°.
    Axial ratio (AR)
    Long axis divided by short axis, always 1 or more. In dB it is 20 log10(AR), a ratio of field amplitudes. 0 dB is a perfect circle.
    Tilt angle τ
    The angle of the ellipse's long axis, measured up from the horizontal.
    RHCP and LHCP
    Right- and left-hand circular polarisation. Point your thumb along the travel direction; the hand whose fingers curl the way the field turns names the sense.
    Polarisation loss factor (PLF)
    The fraction of arriving power the receiving antenna can accept, from 1 (perfect) down to 0 (crossed).
    Polarisation loss
    The same thing in decibels: −10 log10(PLF). A PLF of 0.5 is 3.01 dB of loss.
    Cross-polar discrimination (XPD)
    How much better an antenna hears the polarisation it wants than the one at right angles. Real systems get 25 to 35 dB.
    Dipole
    The simplest antenna: a straight rod, linearly polarised along its own length.
