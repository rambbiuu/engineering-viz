    Model assumptions and the rules of thumb

    —

    Directivity is a numerical integral of the plotted pattern, D = 2 / ∫0π |F(θ)|2 sin θ dθ, by Simpson's rule over 20 000 intervals. Nothing here is read from a table of typical gains.

    Three trades live in the five buttons. Concentration is the only source of gain: a dipole spreads power over nearly the whole sky for 2.15 dBi, a 6λ aperture squeezes it into ten degrees. Aperture in wavelengths is what counts — hold 6λ and sweep the frequency: the pattern never moves, only the metal shrinks. And sidelobes are the price of a hard-edged aperture: a uniform line or array leaks at −13.26 dB however large it is, a round aperture reaches −17.57 dB for free, and anything better costs gain and beamwidth through tapering.

    Words used here
    Wavelength λ
    Distance from one wave peak to the next, λ = c/f. At 2.4 GHz it is 12.5 cm. Antenna behaviour depends on size in wavelengths, never on size in metres alone.
    Radiation pattern
    How much power the antenna sends in each direction, relative to its own strongest direction.
    Isotropic radiator
    An imaginary antenna radiating equally in every direction. It cannot be built, but it is the yardstick for gain.
    Directivity D
    Peak power density divided by the average over all directions. A pure shape number, ignoring heat lost in the antenna.
    Radiation efficiency η
    The fraction of the fed power that leaves as radio waves. The rest becomes heat in the conductor, dielectric and matching network.
    Gain G, in dBi
    G = ηD, in decibels relative to the isotropic radiator. The "i" means isotropic. A link budget uses gain, because it counts the losses.
    Half-power beamwidth, HPBW
    The angle across the main lobe between the two directions where the power has fallen 3 dB below the peak.
    Sidelobe
    Any lobe other than the main one, quoted in decibels below the peak. The main cause of interference into neighbouring links.
    Null
    A direction of zero radiation between lobes. Point a null at an interferer and it disappears.
    Boresight
    Mechanical boresight is the direction the structure is built to point; electrical boresight is where the beam actually peaks. The angle between them is boresight error.
    Effective aperture Ae
    The area of wavefront the antenna effectively collects, Ae = Gλ2/4π. Even a wire has one.
    Array factor
    The pattern a set of point sources makes purely by interference of their feeds. The real pattern is the array factor times the element pattern.
