Antenna basics — shapes, beams and boresight

An antenna does not radiate equally in all directions; its shape decides where the energy goes. The beam pattern is the map of that preference, and everything engineers argue about — gain, beamwidth, sidelobes, front-to-back ratio, where boresight actually points — is read off that one map. Pick a shape and watch its physical form, its radiation, and its pattern together.

The boresight distinction is the subtle one. Mechanical boresight is where the metal physically aims — rotate the antenna and everything turns with it. Electrical boresight is where the beam actually peaks, which for a phased array you move by changing feed phases while the hardware sits still. On a fixed antenna the two coincide by definition; on an array they part company, and on any real antenna a manufacturing or cable error puts a small permanent offset between them — the boresight error you calibrate out. Try Array, leave mechanical at 0°, and steer electrically: the metal never moves but the beam swings, exactly the trick the phased-array tool does in detail.
