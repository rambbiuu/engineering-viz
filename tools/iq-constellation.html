IQ constellation explorer

A receiver reduces every symbol it hears to one dot: how much of the carrier (I) and how much of the 90°-shifted carrier (Q) it contains. Plot the dots and the modulation scheme appears as a pattern of clusters — the constellation. Clean channel: tight clusters. Real channel: noise blurs them, a phase error rotates them, a frequency error spins them.

Three experiments: drop the SNR to 8 dB on 16QAM and watch errors explode while BPSK stays clean — that is why deep-space links use simple schemes. Give it a phase error and see the whole picture rotate: this is exactly an uncalibrated cable in your antenna array, one fixed phase offset on a channel. Give it a tiny frequency error and the constellation spins continuously — two radios whose local oscillators disagree by a fraction of a hertz. Receivers run carrier-tracking loops precisely to freeze this spin.
