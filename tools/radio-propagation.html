Radio propagation across the whole spectrum

A radio wave has several ways of reaching the far end, and wavelength decides which one wins. Sweep the frequency and watch the winner change — the same watts reach a submarine at 20 kHz and die in a car park at 60 GHz.

If decibels and link budgets are new, start with link budget. If clearance and Fresnel zones are the puzzle, see Fresnel clearance.

An everyday analogy. Bass from a neighbour’s party bends round the wall while the singer’s consonants do not — long waves bend around obstacles, short waves are stopped by them.

The arithmetic behind the cards, with your numbers in it
    Glossary
    Decibel (dB)
    A ratio on a logarithmic scale: 10× more power is 10 dB, 100× is 20 dB. Losses add up in dB instead of multiplying.
    Isotropic antenna
    An imaginary antenna that radiates equally in every direction. It is the reference every real antenna gain is quoted against, hence dBi.
    Effective aperture Ae
    The area of the incoming wavefront an antenna actually collects, in m2. For an isotropic antenna it is λ2/4π, so it shrinks as the frequency rises.
    Free-space loss
    The fall in power between two isotropic antennas in empty space, purely from the wave spreading out over a bigger and bigger sphere.
    k factor
    The number the earth’s radius is multiplied by to account for the atmosphere bending the ray downwards. The usual median is 4/3; it swings from about 1/2 to infinity with the weather.
    Radio horizon
    How far a ray can go before the earth’s curve gets in the way, √(2kaH) with the effective radius ka.
    Diffraction
    The bending of a wave around an obstacle, including the earth itself. It fills the shadow behind the obstacle, but at a price in dB.
    Troposcatter
    Using turbulent patches of air about 10 km up as a very poor mirror. It works far past the horizon and costs 200 dB or more.
    Breakpoint db
    The distance where the direct and ground-reflected rays stop taking turns reinforcing each other and settle into cancellation. Beyond it, path loss steepens from d−2 to d−4.
    foF2
    The critical frequency of the F2 layer: the highest frequency the ionosphere returns when the wave is fired straight up. It is measured, not predicted, and swings with time of day and the solar cycle.
    MUF
    Maximum usable frequency: the highest frequency the ionosphere returns for a given hop length. Higher than foF2 because a glancing ray is turned more easily than a vertical one.
    Fa
    External noise figure: how many dB the noise arriving from outside the antenna sits above the thermal floor kT0b.
    Polarisation
    The direction the electric field points. Rain drops are flattened as they fall, so they intercept a horizontal field more effectively than a vertical one.
    Takeaways, and where this is simplified

    Three things in here are worth taking away. First, “high frequencies do not travel as far” is only true if you hold the antenna size fixed in wavelengths — switch section 2 to fixed dishes and the slope reverses, which is exactly why satellite links keep climbing in frequency. Second, the atmosphere is not a gentle taper but a landscape of walls and windows, so the choice between 60 and 94 GHz is a factor of thirty in dB per kilometre. Third, beyond the horizon nothing stops abruptly: diffraction hands over to troposcatter, and the handover point moves with frequency, antenna height and the weather’s k factor.

    Where this is simplified, it says so on the panel. The four scenes in section 1 are schematic except for the line-of-sight one. The gaseous absorption curve is a logarithmic interpolation through anchor points computed from the full ITU-R P.676-13 line-by-line model at 1013.25 hPa, 15 °C and 7.5 g/m3, not the line-by-line sum itself. Diffraction assumes a smooth earth with no terrain and average ground; troposcatter assumes smooth-earth horizon angles and the median 50 % of time. The ionosphere is one F2 layer at a 300 km virtual height. Surface-wave range has no closed-form model in ITU-R P.368, so this tool does not print one.
