Any repeating wave — square, triangle, sawtooth — is secretly a stack of plain sine waves. Add them one at a time and watch the target shape appear. This is the idea underneath every spectrum, filter, and FFT you will ever meet.

RMS difference from the target; the overshoot spikes at sharp jumps never fully disappear (Gibbs effect)

Watch what each shape demands: the square only wants odd harmonics, fading slowly (1/n) — that is why it needs so many and why its corners ring. The triangle also wants odd ones but they fade fast (1/n²), so a handful is enough. The sawtooth wants every harmonic. A sharp edge in time always means a long tail in frequency — the same trade your antenna receiver faces reading fast signals.
