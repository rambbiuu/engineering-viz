Worked example — the textbook 1 km hop
  1. 1 km at 150 MHz, so f = 0.15 GHz. Both masts 27 m, a 21 m ridge at mid-path, d₁ = d₂ = 0.5 km.
  2. F₁ = 17.3 √(0.5 × 0.5 / (0.15 × 1)) = 17.3 × 1.291 = 22.33 m.
  3. Bulge at k = 1.33: 0.5 × 0.5 / (12.75 × 1.333) = 0.01 m — negligible at 1 km, dominant at 100 km.
  4. Clearance 27 − 21 − 0.01 = 5.99 m, which is 5.99 / 22.33 = 0.27 F₁.
  5. ν = −√2 × 0.27 = −0.379.
  6. Exact loss J(−0.379) = 2.80 dB; the P.526 closed form gives 2.88 dB.
  7. 0.27 F₁ is under 0.6 F₁, so the path fails the rule even though the ridge is in plain sight.

An everyday analogy

Slide a book up towards a torch beam: the light dims before the book reaches the straight line, and the shadow's soft edge shows faint bright and dark bands. Radio does the same.

Where this model is simplified

The obstacle is one ideal knife edge, so a broad rounded hill or two ridges in a row lose more than shown; ITU-R P.526 gives the corrections. The reflection panel assumes a single specular bounce with the reflection magnitude set by the slider rather than computed from grazing angle, polarisation and sea state. Atmospheric absorption, rain and antenna patterns are not modelled.

Glossary
Fresnel zone
The region around the straight path within which a scattered ray arrives less than half a wavelength late. The first zone carries most of the energy.
F₁
Radius of the first Fresnel zone at a point along the path, in metres. Widest at mid-path, zero at each mast.
Clearance
How far the sight line passes above the obstacle top. Negative means the far antenna is hidden.
Diffraction
The bending of a wave around an edge. It is why a blocked path still delivers some signal.
ν (nu)
Dimensionless diffraction parameter, ν = −√2 · clearance / F₁. Positive means blocked.
J(ν)
Knife-edge diffraction loss in decibels. J(0) = 6.02 dB, the loss at grazing.
dB, decibel
A ratio on a logarithmic scale. 3 dB is half the power, 10 dB a tenth.
Earth bulge
Height by which the curved earth rises above the straight chord. About 147 m mid-path on 100 km at k = 4/3.
k factor
Factor multiplying the earth's radius because the atmosphere bends radio downward. Median 4/3; falls toward 1/2 in sub-refractive air, doubling the bulge.
Specular reflection
A mirror-like bounce off a smooth surface at an equal angle. Sea does this; rough ground scatters.
