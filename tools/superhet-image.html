    Why a later filter cannot help

    Two people shout from 3 m either side of you: equal distance, so you cannot tell which side spoke. The mixer does the same thing to frequency, and once both signals sit on the IF nothing downstream can separate them.

    Intermodulation is a different failure. The image is a frequency problem and a filter fixes it; intermodulation is a level problem and no filter fixes it. Push a mixer hard enough and two tones going in produce sums and differences at every combination n·f1 ± m·f2. Even orders land near DC or near twice the carrier and filter out easily; the third-order pair 2f1−f2 and 2f2−f1 falls inside the very passband you were protecting, and rises 3 dB for every 1 dB of drive, so the clear space closes at 2 dB per dB.

    Three trades live in these sliders. Raising the IF pushes the image further down the filter skirt and buys rejection almost free, but the IF filter then needs a smaller fractional bandwidth for the same channel — which is why 10.7 MHz suits an FM band 20 MHz wide and is hopeless at 2.4 GHz. Narrowing the front-end filter or adding poles buys rejection too, but a tracking filter that narrow must stay tuned as you turn the dial. And once fRF/IF passes roughly 10 to 20, no single conversion works: the answer is two, a high first IF to kill the image and a low second IF to do the selecting.

    Simplifications: the front end is an ideal Butterworth with no insertion loss, its stopband floor is a control rather than a measured leakage path, the mixer is treated as having 0 dB conversion gain so input and output intercepts coincide, and OIP5 is assumed to sit a fixed distance below OIP3 because no general relation exists.

    Glossary
    Superheterodyne (superhet)
    A receiver that shifts every incoming channel to one fixed frequency before filtering and detecting it, instead of building a tunable filter for each channel.
    Mixer
    A deliberately non-linear device fed with the incoming signal and the local oscillator. Its output contains the sum and difference of their frequencies.
    Local oscillator (LO)
    The tone the receiver generates itself. Tuning the radio means changing fLO.
    Intermediate frequency (IF)
    The fixed frequency everything is shifted to: fIF = |fRF − fLO|. Common values are 0.455, 10.7 and 70 MHz.
    Image frequency
    The second input frequency that produces the same IF, sitting 2 × IF away from the wanted signal on the other side of the LO.
    High-side / low-side injection
    Whether the LO is placed above the wanted signal (image above) or below it (image below).
    Image rejection
    The extra attenuation the front end applies at the image frequency compared with the wanted frequency, in dB. 60 dB means an equally strong image arrives 60 dB weaker.
    Preselector / front-end filter
    The bandpass filter between antenna and mixer. The only place the image can be removed.
    Butterworth order
    How many poles the filter has. |H(f)|² = 1/(1 + off2N): flat in the passband, and 20N dB steeper per decade of offset.
    dB and dBm
    dB is a ratio on a logarithmic scale: 10 dB is ten times the power, 3 dB is roughly double. dBm is an absolute power referred to one milliwatt, so 0 dBm = 1 mW and −70 dBm = 0.1 pW.
    Intermodulation (IM)
    New frequencies created when two or more signals pass through something non-linear together.
    Third-order product (IM3)
    The pair 2f1−f2 and 2f2−f1. It lands next to the original tones and rises 3 dB per 1 dB of drive.
    OIP3
    Output third-order intercept point, in dBm: the extrapolated output level where the fundamental and third-order lines cross.
    Spurious-free window
    How far the third-order product sits below the wanted output, in dB. Equal to 2 × (OIP3 − drive level).
    1 dB compression point
    The output level at which real gain has sagged 1 dB below the straight line. A rule of thumb puts it about 10 dB below OIP3.
