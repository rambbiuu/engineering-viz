        Why the order matters

        A cascade is a chain of photocopies: specks on the first copy are enlarged by every machine after it, grit on the last barely shows. Noise is set by the front of the chain, headroom by the back — every decibel of loss ahead of the first amplifier lands on the noise figure one for one, while the last, highest-level stage saturates first. Two simplifications: passive blocks sit at the 290 K reference temperature, and the intercept sum takes the worst case of in-phase distortion.

        Words used on this page
        dB (decibel)
        A ratio on a logarithmic scale: 10 log10 of a power ratio. +3 dB is roughly double the power, −3 dB roughly half.
        dBm
        An absolute power in dB relative to one milliwatt. 0 dBm is 1 mW; −100 dBm is 0.1 picowatt, a normal size for a received radio signal.
        Thermal noise
        The random voltage every warm resistor produces. Its power is k T B watts, which at room temperature is −174 dBm in every hertz of bandwidth.
        Noise bandwidth B
        The width in hertz that decides how much of that noise the receiver lets through. Doubling it lets in 3.01 dB more noise.
        Noise factor F
        The number of times a block worsens the signal-to-noise ratio: F = SNRin / SNRout, as plain ratios. F is never less than 1.
        Noise figure NF
        The same thing in dB: NF = 10 log10 F. NF = 3 dB means the block throws away half the signal-to-noise ratio.
        Noise temperature Te
        The same noise as a temperature: Te = (F − 1) × 290 K.
        Passive block
        A block with no power supply — cable, filter, diode mixer. It can only attenuate, and its noise figure in dB equals its loss in dB.
        LNA (low-noise amplifier)
        The first amplifier in a receiver, chosen for the lowest noise figure rather than the most gain.
        Friis cascade formula
        F = F1 + (F2−1)/G1 + (F3−1)/(G1G2) + … Every term is a plain ratio, never dB.
        Noise floor
        The receiver's own noise power in the wanted bandwidth, in dBm. Any signal below it is buried.
        Sensitivity
        The weakest signal, in dBm, that still arrives with the required SNR. Noise floor plus required SNR.
        OIP3 and IIP3
        Output and input third-order intercept points, in dBm. They measure how well a block copes with strong signals. IIP3 = OIP3 − gain.
        SFDR
        Spurious-free dynamic range: the span in dB between the weakest signal a receiver can hear and the strongest it can hear cleanly.
